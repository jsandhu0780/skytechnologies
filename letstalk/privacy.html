Let's Talk Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 3, 2026

Let's Talk is run by Jashan Sandhu in Brampton, Ontario, Canada ("we"). This policy explains what we collect, why, who handles it and the choices you have. Jashan Sandhu is responsible for privacy at Let's Talk. Questions: jsandhu@skytechnologies.ca.

What we can't read

In current versions of the app, these are end-to-end encrypted. They're locked on the sender's phone and only unlocked on the phones of people in the chat, so we can't read them on our servers:

  • Message text, file names and link previews
  • Photos, videos, voice messages and documents sent in chats
  • Shared locations and contacts, and the contents of events and polls

There are two exceptions. Messages sent before encryption was added to the app, or from an older version, may be stored unencrypted. And if you report a message, you send us a copy (see "Reports").

What we collect and can read

  • Account: your email address, display name, username and About line. Passwords and two-step verification are handled by our sign-in provider, and passwords aren't stored in readable form.
  • Profile and group photos: these are stored at a web address that isn't protected, so anyone who has the link can see them.
  • Statuses: text, captions, photos and videos, and who viewed them. These aren't end-to-end encrypted.
  • Activity: who is in each chat, group names and descriptions, who sent a message and when, its type, length, size and file extension, read times, when you were last online, reactions, poll votes and starred messages.
  • Calls: who called whom, the type of call, when it happened, how long it lasted and any scheduled call titles.
  • Device details: notification tokens and your phone's public encryption key.
  • Technical logs: our service providers may keep logs such as IP addresses to run and secure their services.

We use this to provide the app, deliver messages and calls, keep accounts secure and deal with abuse. We don't show ads, sell your data or use analytics tools.

Calls

Voice and video calls are encrypted on the way to and from the call server, but they aren't end-to-end encrypted. Our call provider's servers relay the audio and video while a call is happening, and receive your display name and account ID. The app has no call recording feature.

Phone permissions

  • Camera and microphone: for calls, photos, videos and voice messages.
  • Photos: to send what you choose, and to save to Photos if you turn that on.
  • Contacts: your phone's own picker opens when you share a contact. We don't read your address book.
  • Location: only when you choose to share it in a chat.
  • Calendar: only to add an event when you ask.
  • Notifications: for messages and calls.

On your phone

Your private encryption key stays on your phone and isn't included in backups. A new phone can't read older messages. Photos and files you've opened may stay in the app's cache, and anything you save to Photos, share or export is no longer protected by the app.

Who handles data for us

  • Supabase: accounts, database, file storage and live updates.
  • LiveKit: voice and video calls.
  • Expo, Apple and, on Android, Google: notifications. They see the sender's name, the group name, scheduled call titles, a label such as "New message" or "Photo" and the details needed to deliver it, but not encrypted content. Apple also sees the caller's name for incoming calls on iPhone.
  • Apple Maps: maps and addresses for shared locations on iPhone.
  • Websites you share: when you send a link, your phone fetches the preview from that website, which sees your IP address.

Our database and files are stored in the United States (Ohio). Calls connect through the LiveKit servers nearest to the people on the call, which may be in another country. Data held outside Canada may be accessed by courts, law enforcement and national security authorities under the laws there.

Reports

If you report a message or status, your phone sends us a copy of it, including any photo, video or file, along with who sent it, your account and your reason. We use this to review abuse and enforce our Terms of Use. We keep report copies for up to 90 days after a report is resolved, or longer if the law requires it. If you report a person or group from their profile or info page, no messages are sent.

How long we keep data

  • Messages stay until they're deleted, they disappear under the chat's timer or the sender deletes their account.
  • Statuses disappear after 24 hours. Their stored photos and videos are removed the next time the person who posted them opens the app.
  • Files from disappearing messages and opened view once messages are removed the next time the sender opens the app. These files stay encrypted until then.
  • Deleting your account removes your profile, the messages you sent and your files. Group photos you added stay with the group. Backups may be kept for up to 30 days, and reports as described above.
  • Other people keep what they've already saved or exported.

Your choices and rights

  • You can edit your profile, turn off read receipts and notification previews, block people and delete your account in the app.
  • You can ask to access or correct your personal information, or ask questions about how it's handled, by emailing jsandhu@skytechnologies.ca. We reply within 30 days.
  • You can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada, or to the privacy regulator where you live.

Security

We use end-to-end encryption, encrypted connections, database access rules and optional two-step verification. No system is perfectly secure. To check who you're talking to, compare security codes in Chat, then their name, then Encryption.

Children

Let's Talk isn't intended for anyone under 16. Contact us if you think a child has created an account, and we'll delete it.

Changes

We'll update this page when this policy changes, and tell you in the app if a change is significant.
